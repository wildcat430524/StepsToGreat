# 05_Conversational Entry

> Purpose: after understanding needs, begin every content block with a conversational introduction, then proceed to explanation, practice, and filing.
> Chinese version: [`05_对话导入.md`](./05_对话导入.md)

## 1. When to use it

First understand the subject, purpose, background, deadline, and available time. Collect missing information together without re-asking known facts. Confirm needs in chat without requiring the student to fill a profile. Agree on a provisional exploration direction when the goal is undecided; do not invent a goal.

After that, begin **every new content block** with a conversational introduction: initial exploration, lesson 1, later lessons, and new blocks within a lesson. A block is a teaching unit with one small objective and usually 1-2 related concepts, not every paragraph, exercise, or reply. Do not revert to publishing long documents first after initial onboarding, or repeat full requirements gathering for each block.

Honor explicit requests for direct explanation, placement, skipping, or grading. For an existing lesson or unresolved round, read the handoff and answer document first. A short situation may help resume that same topic and round; it does not authorize a new lesson or another placement test. Starting the next block's introduction still requires the preceding round or lesson to meet its advancement criteria.

Project development, upgrades, and reviews are engineering requests. A blank profile does not require starting tutoring.

## 2. How to begin

Before the situation, separately show module mastery progress and the current concrete skill checklist per [`06_小目标进度.en.md`](./06_小目标进度.en.md). Unknown module scope is pending; do not delay entry to invent a bar. Do not reveal future prompts. Ask one current question, then immediately name the actual correct step and its practical result, acknowledging help honestly. Entry observations remain provisional, without mastery ticks or module-bar advances.

**A concrete situation -> something to accomplish -> a small obstacle -> one question the student can answer now.**

- Use the student's actual problem when available. Otherwise use a short hypothetical situation related to their subject and goal; a few sentences usually suffice.
- Ask **one small question at a time**: a number, a sentence, one action, or a judgment. Do not give the algorithm and then ask for repetition, or hide several subquestions in one prompt.
- Begin with observation, common sense, or existing experience. Do not require installation, terminology, or a complete large exercise. Easy entry does not require correct answers; a wrong answer must still allow participation.
- Each question should build understanding or yield diagnostic evidence. Avoid empty agreement or readiness checks.
- "Do you want totals, duplicate detection, or monthly summaries?" is a feature menu, not a problem introduction. Necessary goal clarification is allowed, but should not substitute for a situation.

**Example: the student wants Excel for shop sales records; this block concerns quantity and unit price.**

> Just before closing, a shop owner sends over two hundred sales rows and asks how much was sold this week. You reach for a calculator. Each row contains the quantity sold and its unit price.
>
> Start with one sale: three drinks at 12 each, with no other charges. How much was that sale worth?

Only after the answer, ask a next question such as "What if there were five drinks?" Use the explanation and variation to introduce quantity times price, formulas, and repeated calculations. Do not publish the whole sequence upfront or infer Excel proficiency from an answer of 36.

## 3. Responding and stopping

Respond to the student's actual answer, then explain this small step. Praise a specific idea rather than giving generic congratulations. If the student struggles, clarify the situation, supply a minimal prerequisite example, or build on a correct part of their answer.

Adapt from an intuitive answer to a short reason, a variation, and independent application. Record prompted answers as prompted; repetition of a tutor-provided solution is not independent evidence. Shrink the current problem when stuck instead of switching topics to hide the gap.

After a meaningful small closure, offer a natural stopping point and the next small step. Do not ask permission to continue after every answer or turn completion into an automatic commitment to more work. Continue when requested; issue no new question when the student explicitly stops.

## 4. Transition to formal study

Once the block's concrete problem is established and the student has attempted one small step, use the revealed need to move into explanation and practice, then organize the necessary teaching and answer documents. One situation and one or two follow-ups will often suffice; this is not a fixed quota. Respect requests to remain in chat and explain that no formal mastery record exists yet.

Overall placement is for initial routing. Later blocks follow the existing route and actual answers, with local prerequisite checks when needed, not repeated overall placement.

If initial exploration already introduced lesson 1's same block, continue that situation rather than introducing it again. When resuming an introduced block, follow the next small step from the handoff.

Apply [`01_摸底剧本.en.md`](./01_摸底剧本.en.md). Entry questions may count toward the 2-8 diagnostic probes only when their exact prompts, original answers, and hint history are preserved and diagnostically relevant. Add only missing probes. Basic arithmetic does not establish software skills; mark untested capabilities as untested. Publish lesson 1 after placement and the route are filed.

## 5. Chat and file boundaries

| Stage | Records and assessment |
|---|---|
| Conversational introduction | Defer this block's new teaching and answer documents; workspace initialization may also wait on first use. Read existing rules, profile, and current lesson first. Feedback on visible answers is an initial observation, never formal mastery or a three-dimension pass. |
| Natural stop | Leave a short chat recap of the actual step, independent or prompted performance, unresolved point, and next step. An unsaved recap cannot guarantee resumption in another chat. Do not reconstruct invisible old answers. |
| Formal transition | On first use preserve prompts, original answers, and hints in placement records, re-read, establish the starting point and route. For later blocks append entry answers and hints to the owning lesson's answer document and add its explanation to the teaching guide; do not create a separate lesson per block. Label entry records as informal feedback and verify independently in formal rounds. Initial observations are not mastery. |
| Formal answers submitted in chat | Read the current answer document, append the student's unchanged answer under its question, identify it as a chat submission, then re-read before complete round assessment. Missing answers remain pending; do not answer for the student. |

If durable progress is requested, on first use append prompts, answers, hints, and untested scope to `我的学习/学科/<subject>/00-摸底测试.md`, marked "entry record; placement incomplete". Initialize missing profile/rules and set the handoff to placement, leaving lesson number and course documents unpublished. Within an existing lesson, append to its answer document. For an unpublished new lesson, create its necessary document pair and index, marked "introduction in progress; formal round not issued", without issuing the next round's questions. Create no second authoritative state table or mastery claim. The tutor handles filing.

With no workspace and only an entry interaction, defer directory selection and initialization until filing is needed. Never put student data in the skill installation directory.

## 6. Sources and verification

Stories may be explicitly hypothetical; do not present them as real cases or research. Knowledge explanations still need the sources required by the tutor protocol. Read the student's material index and actual content; report unreadable sources rather than teaching around them from memory.

Check that the entry presents a concrete problem, asks only the current question, responds to the actual answer, records hints honestly, allows stopping, and retains formal evidence and mastery assessment. Passing protocol checks does not establish improved retention or learning outcomes.
