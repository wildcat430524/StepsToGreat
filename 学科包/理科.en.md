## 1. Three-dimension definitions

| Dimension | Name | What it judges | What a typical ✅ looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① | **Conceptual understanding** | Whether they understand the definition, the conditions, and the boundaries of applicability | Knows "derivative = rate of change" and knows it does not exist at a non-differentiable point |
| ② | **Derivation & calculation** | Whether the steps hold, the result is correct, and the units are right | Every step from the given conditions to the conclusion has a basis, the numbers are substituted correctly, the units are consistent |
| ③ | **Formal correctness** (derivation conventions) | Symbols, written form, dimensions, logical chain are **structurally** correct | Symbols consistent throughout, equals signs in the right direction, no skipped steps, units labelled, conditions stated |

> **The ② vs ③ distinction**: ② judges "**is the conclusion right**", ③ judges "**is the written process well-formed**".
> The result is right but steps are badly skipped and symbols are chaotic → ② ✅ / ③ ⚠️. This is the most common scoring disagreement in this subject, and **the two must be marked separately**.

### 1.1 Boundary cases: which dimension does this error belong to? (contested ones only)

The general arbitration principle is in `协议/00_导师协议.en.md` §3 (**said it wrong → ①; thought it wrong → ②; wrote it wrong → ③**).
High-frequency boundaries in science (**this subject disagrees most often**):

| Case | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Incomplete case analysis** (absolute value / quadratic roots / piecewise, only one case written) | **② ❌** | Incomplete reasoning → a "thinking" problem. ⚠️ Previously filed under ③ — **corrected** |
| **Right conclusion but badly skipped steps** | **③ ⚠️** | The reasoning holds; the process is merely not written out. See the "step-skipping criteria" below |
| **Inconsistent symbols** (`v` then `u` for one quantity) | **③ ⚠️** | A form issue; the reasoning itself is fine |
| **Units omitted** | **③ ⚠️** | A missing formality; **if it makes the result unverifiable → ③ ❌** |
| **Wrong dimensions / unit arithmetic** (`m/s + m`, cm mixed with m) | **③ ❌** | Structural error — the result is **necessarily** wrong |
| **Vector direction omitted / wrong** | **③ ⚠️**; **② ❌** when it flips the sign of the answer | An omitted arrow is form; a wrong resulting direction is logic |
| **A formula used without stating its applicability** | **③ ⚠️**; **① ❌** when the condition doesn't hold and it is used anyway | Omission is form; **using the wrong condition** is conceptual |
| **Treating "necessary" as "sufficient"** | **② ❌** | The chain does not hold |
| **Arithmetic slip with a correct method** (3×7 written as 20, continues from 21) | **no dimension deducted** (note it) | A slip, not a lack of understanding. See "slip vs not understanding" below |
| **Unit criteria on a pure maths problem** | **mark `— N/A`** | No physical dimensions; this pack authorises N/A here |

### 1.2 The "step-skipping" threshold (the most argued point in this subject)

③ judges only "**were the steps that must be written actually written**". The criteria are that **these four step classes appear**; missing any → **③ ⚠️**:

| Must appear | Example (a mechanics problem) |
|---|---|
| **① Basis** (which law / formula, stated) | "From Newton's second law…" |
| **② Model equation** (the original equation with the conditions substituted, not jumping to the last line) | `F − f = ma` |
| **③ Intermediate quantity** (at least one physically meaningful intermediate result) | solve for `a` first, then `v` |
| **④ Conclusion + conditions / units** | result with units; all cases in a case split listed |

- **Missing 1** → **③ ⚠️** (a direct fix can supply it)
- **Missing 2+ , or skipping "② model equation"** → **③ ❌** (there is no checkable derivation left)
- **Back-substitution is not the student's obligation** (a mechanical check the tutor may prompt, not a dimension)

> **In one line**: **"Could someone else reproduce the process from what is written?"** Yes → no deduction; No → ③ ⚠️.

### 1.3 Telling a "slip" from "not understanding"

The previous version only said "distinguish them"; here are **concrete actions**:

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| **Ask "how did you get this step?"** | Can explain the approach (just miscalculated) → **a slip, no dimension deduction**, note it; cannot, or the approach is itself wrong → **②/①** |
| **Look at the error distribution** | One occurrence → likely a slip; **repeated at the same place** → an understanding problem |
| **See whether it self-corrects** | Fixes it after "take another look at this step" → slip; still wrong → understanding |

**Handling**:
- **Slip** → no dimension deduction, but note "1 arithmetic slip this round (N×N)" in `01_学生回答.md`, and record a **habit observation** in the 📊 remarks (3 slips of the same kind in a row → add a targeted reminder to ⏳).
- **Not understanding** → run the normal not-mastered loop (Socratic → re-assess).

## 2. Dimension ③ criteria

### ❌ Structural errors (must lose points)

> ⚠️ **Attribution corrected per §1.1**: each item now states the dimension it **actually** belongs to.
> The previous version filed them all under "③ structural errors", which was a misattribution (see
> [`../docs/simulations/00-优化汇总.md`](../docs/simulations/00-优化汇总.md) C1 and science A8).

| Error | Actual dimension | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Wrong dimensions / unit arithmetic** (`m/s + m`, cm mixed with m) | **③ Formal** ❌ | The result is necessarily wrong — genuinely structural |
| **Units omitted (making the work unverifiable)** | **③ Formal** ❌ | "Unverifiable" is the key; mere omission that doesn't block checking → ⚠️ |
| **Inconsistent symbols** (`v` then `u` for one quantity) | **③ Formal** ⚠️ | Form; the reasoning is fine |
| **Missing conditions** (formula used without stating applicability) | **③ Formal** ⚠️ | Omission is form; **using the wrong condition** is **① Concept** ❌ |
| **Excessive logical jumps** (A straight to C, middle B unexplained) | **③ Formal** ⚠️ | Use the step-skipping criteria in §1.2; skipping the "model equation" → ❌ |
| **Misuse of the equals sign** | **③ Formal** ⚠️ | A writing-convention issue |
| **Incomplete case analysis** | **② Logic** ❌ | Incomplete reasoning, **not** a form issue (previously misattributed) |
| **Inappropriate approximation** | **② Logic** ❌ | The chain does not hold (previously misattributed) |
| **Treating "necessary" as "sufficient"** | **② Logic** ❌ | Reasoning error |
| **Arithmetic slip** | **no dimension deducted** | See §1.3 — ask "how did you get this step?" first |

### ⚠️ Exemptable (no points lost)

- A numerical slip with a correct process (e.g. 3×7 written as 20, but the rest continues from 21)
- Unit written in full vs abbreviated inconsistently (`米` and `m`)
- Hand-drawn diagrams not well-formed (rough sketches, crooked arrows)
- Preference for a given number of significant figures
- Using a different but equivalent symbol convention (e.g. `ln` and `log` under their respective conventions)

> **Key point**: mathematical symbols written by hand in Markdown often go wrong (a missing `^`, a subscript written as `_`, etc.) → as long as the intent is clear, it is not an error.

## 3. High-frequency problem checklist

1. Memorising formulas without understanding the conditions of applicability
2. Skipped steps (assuming that because you understand, everyone does — this loses marks in exams)
3. Chaotic symbols (the same letter standing for two quantities)
4. Inconsistent units (centimetres and metres mixed in one calculation)
5. Missed cases (absolute values, roots of a quadratic, piecewise functions)
6. Ignored boundary conditions (zero denominator, negative under a square root)
7. Treating "necessary" as "sufficient" (the converse doesn't hold)
8. Calculation slips (not a comprehension problem, a computation error — keep these separate)
9. Conclusion without units / without direction (vectors)
10. No checking habit (substituting back into the original expression to verify)

## 4. Question forms

| Round | Question type | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | **Concept questions** (1–2) | "What are the conditions of applicability for X? Give one case where it doesn't apply" — tests boundaries |
| Round 2 | **Calculation questions** (2) | Must **swap the data and add a boundary**; cannot be isomorphic to the worked example |
| Round 3 | **Synthesis question** (1) | Needs two or more steps, using this lesson plus earlier knowledge |

### This subject's question cost: **medium → heavy**

Per the "question cost scaling" in `协议/00_导师协议.en.md` §2:

| Question type | Cost | Per round |
|---|---|---|
| Concept question (conditions / boundaries) | Light | up to 3 |
| Single-step calculation / unit conversion | Light–medium | 2–3 |
| **Multi-step derivation** (with case splitting) | **Heavy** | **1–2** |
| **Synthesis question** (2+ steps + case split + back-substitution) | **Heavy** | **1** |

> **Why**: one science question typically contains "set up → derive → split cases → back-substitute" — a dozen lines of formulae.
> Across the 8 simulations ([`../docs/simulations/07-理科-数学微积分.md`](../docs/simulations/07-理科-数学微积分.md),
> [`08-理科-物理.md`](../docs/simulations/08-理科-物理.md)): two derivations in one round make the tutor's reply
> unmanageable and the student loses the thread.
>
> **Allowed**: "one round = one full derivation" is fully compliant.
> **Hint**: a multi-step physics problem also needs a force diagram, so prefer 1 question.

> **Send only 1–3 questions per round, and send the next round only when this round is all ✅** (see §2 of `协议/00_导师协议.md`).

**What a good science question looks like**:
- Has a unique correct answer (or a finite set)
- Hides one boundary / trap (zero denominator, degenerate case)
- Can be solved without tools not yet learned
- Tests "do they understand" rather than "do they remember"

**Forbidden**:
- ❌ Pure recall questions ("write out formula XX")
- ❌ Questions that need out-of-syllabus tools
- ❌ Questions with huge computational volume but trivial thinking (that is physical labour)

## 5. Verification methods

1. **Substitute back**: put the answer back into the original expression — does it hold?
2. **Dimensional check**: are the units of the result right?
3. **Limit check**: take a quantity to 0 or infinity — is the result sensible?
4. **Change the conditions**: change a condition slightly — how does the answer move? Why?
5. **Explain the principle**: why is this step a valid transformation?

> "Got the answer" may be a guess; **"can explain why every step holds" is mastery**.

### How to use the Feynman technique in science and math

| Topics worth a Feynman question | Not worth it |
|---|---|
| A theorem's **conditions of applicability and intuition**: why a derivative can describe a rate of change | The formula itself (memorise it and you're done) |
| Physical pictures: why inertia exists, why entropy increases | Specific integration techniques |
| Why this formula looks like this (and not otherwise) | Unit conversion |
| The essential difference between two concepts (e.g. "mass" vs "weight") | What value a constant has |

**Special constraints for science/math Feynman questions**:

- ❌ No formulas allowed ("use words, no mathematical symbols") — writing the formula means skipping the understanding
- ✅ **Physical/geometric intuition is mandatory**: "why is the derivative of velocity acceleration? In words."
- ✅ A good wording: "In words someone who **knows no maths at all** could follow, explain what a 'limit' is."

**Typical wording**:

```
No formulas, and these words are banned: <derivative>, <limit>, <differential>

Use an everyday example to explain: why is the idea of "how fast something changes" meaningful?
(Hint: think about the speedometer on a car dashboard — what is it showing?)
```

## 6. No further-reading list

**Do not** hand the student a list like "required reading: <textbook>, chapter 3 §3.2" — see §8 of `协议/00_导师协议.md`:
a book title and chapter number recalled from memory can't be verified, and the student probably doesn't own that textbook.

**All teaching content goes into that lesson's teaching guide**, written out by the tutor (worked examples and derivations included).

**If the student has their own material** (a textbook, lecture notes or past papers inside `资料/`) → **teach directly from what's actually in it**,
and cite "from `<filename>`, `<specific chapter/section/question>`" — that's a real, checkable citation, not a memory recall.
