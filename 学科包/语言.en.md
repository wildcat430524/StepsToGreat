# Subject Pack · Language Learning

> Applies to: foreign-language learning such as English, Japanese, French (speaking, reading, writing, listening, vocabulary, grammar).
> Chinese version: [`语言.md`](./语言.md)

---

## 1. Three-dimension definitions

| Dimension | Name | What it judges | What a typical ✅ looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| ① | **Understanding** | Whether they really read / heard what the original means | Can state the main clause and the modifying relations of a long hard sentence instead of translating word by word |
| ② | **Expression** | Whether the sentences / paragraphs they produce convey the meaning, sound natural, and fit the context | When they want to say "I feel", they use `I'd say` rather than mechanically swapping in `I think` |
| ③ | **Formal correctness** (expression conventions) | Grammar, tense, collocation, spelling are **structurally** correct | Subject-verb agreement, tense harmony, countable vs uncountable, correct preposition collocations |

---

## 2. Dimension ③ criteria

### ❌ Structural errors (must lose points)

- Systematic tense errors (telling a present-day story entirely in the past tense)
- Subject-verb disagreement (`He go`, `The list of items are`)
- Incomplete sentence constituents (no predicate / a subordinate clause with no main clause)
- Collocation errors (`depend of`, `discuss about`, `arrive to`)
- Systematic article misuse (a bare countable singular: `I bought book`)
- Word order errors that affect understanding (question word order, position of modifiers)
- Literal Chinese structures forced into English (`Although..., but...`)

### ⚠️ Exemptable (no points lost, not corrected line by line)

- Case slips (sentence not starting with a capital), missing punctuation
- Obvious typos (`recieve` → `receive`)
- Colloquial contractions in written exercises (`don't` in formal writing)
- Comma / semicolon preference differences (British vs American)
- Word choice that isn't idiomatic but is grammatically correct (that is a dimension ② issue, not ③)

> **Key distinction**: ③ only looks at "**is the structure right**"; "**does it sound natural**" belongs to ②.

---

## 3. High-frequency problem checklist

1. Tense: confusing present perfect with simple past
2. Subject-verb agreement: forgetting the number when a long modifier follows the subject
3. Articles: a / an / the / zero article
4. Prepositions: fixed collocations not sticking (in/on/at for time)
5. Non-finite verbs: base form vs -ing vs past participle
6. Clauses: choosing relative pronouns, word order in noun clauses
7. Vocabulary: recognised but unusable (passive vocabulary ≠ active vocabulary)
8. Long hard sentences: can't find the main clause
9. Chinese-style expression: translating Chinese word order literally
10. Listening: can't hear liaison / weak forms (if the goal includes listening)

## 4. Question forms

| Round | Question type | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | **Comprehension questions** (1–2) | Give one long sentence from a real exam paper → state the main clause / the author's attitude; or translate one sentence |
| Round 2 | **Production questions** (2) | Chinese-to-English translation / rewriting sentences / making sentences with a specified structure |
| Round 3 | **Synthesis question** (1) | Write a short passage (80–120 words) expressing an opinion, using this lesson's structure |

> **Send only 1–3 questions per round, and send the next round only when this round is all ✅** (see §2 of `协议/00_导师协议.md`).

**What a good language question looks like**:
- Has a **single correct answer** or clear scoring points (avoid "it depends on your view")
- Uses this lesson's knowledge points
- Has a real context (not an isolated sentence)

**Forbidden**:
- ❌ Asking "memorise paragraph N of the text"
- ❌ An open-ended essay with no scoring points (the student can't self-check)
- ❌ Twenty spelling words in one go (that is mechanical labour, not teaching)

## 5. Verification methods

1. **Restate**: say that Chinese sentence again in English (without looking at notes)
2. **Rewrite**: express the same meaning with a different structure
3. **Error correction**: give a sentence with a mistake, have them fix it and explain why
4. **Make sentences**: build 3 sentences in different contexts using this lesson's structure
5. **Follow-up question**: why is `have done` used here rather than `did`?

> "Can understand it" is not the same as "can write it"; **only active production counts as mastery**.

---

## 6. How to write further reading

Language-learning material must be precise down to **unit + text/exercise number**:

```markdown
### 📚 Further reading
- Required: <textbook> Unit <N> <unit name> §<text/grammar point> (read before class / compare after class)
- Optional: <textbook> Unit <N>'s <extended reading> (when to come back to it)
- Self-test source for this lesson: <textbook> Unit <N> exercise <question number range>
```

Preferred order: real exam texts > authoritative textbooks > dictionary example sentences (Oxford / Longman) > second-hand explanations.
